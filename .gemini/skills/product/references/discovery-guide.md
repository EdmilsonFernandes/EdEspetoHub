# Discovery Guide

## Purpose

Discovery research helps you understand the problem space before jumping to solutions. The goal is to gather insights about users, their needs, and the context in which they operate.

---

## Research Methods

### 1. User Interviews

**Best for**: Deep understanding of motivations, pain points, behaviors.

**Tips**:
- 30-60 minutes per interview
- 5-8 interviews reveal most patterns
- Record (with permission) for team review
- Use open-ended questions
- Follow up with "why?" and "tell me more"

### 2. Contextual Inquiry

**Best for**: Understanding real workflows and environments.

**Tips**:
- Observe users in their natural environment
- Ask them to narrate their process
- Note workarounds and frustrations
- Look for what they don't say

### 3. Surveys

**Best for**: Quantifying findings across larger populations.

**Tips**:
- Keep short (5-10 questions)
- Use after qualitative research to validate
- Mix closed and open questions
- Avoid leading questions

### 4. Competitive Analysis

**Best for**: Understanding market landscape and alternatives.

**Tips**:
- Identify direct and indirect competitors
- Note strengths and weaknesses
- Look for unmet needs
- Document pricing and positioning

### 5. Data Analysis

**Best for**: Understanding actual behavior at scale.

**Tips**:
- Review usage analytics
- Analyze support tickets
- Look at drop-off points
- Identify power users vs strugglers

## Interview Questions

### Opening (Build Rapport)
- Tell me about your role and responsibilities.
- Walk me through a typical day/week.
- How long have you been doing this?

### Current State
- How do you currently handle [topic]?
- Walk me through the last time you [relevant task].
- What tools or processes do you use?
- Who else is involved?

### Pain Points
- What's the most frustrating part of [process]?
- Where do things typically go wrong?
- What takes longer than it should?
- If you could wave a magic wand, what would you change?

### Workarounds
- Have you found any tricks to make this easier?
- What do you wish you had known earlier?
- Are there any tools you've tried that didn't work?

### Goals & Motivations
- What does success look like for you?
- How is your performance measured?
- What would make your job easier?
- What are you trying to achieve with [task]?

### Context
- When do you typically do this task?
- Where are you when this happens?
- What else is going on at the same time?
- Who do you work with on this?

### Closing
- Is there anything I didn't ask that I should have?
- Anyone else you'd recommend I talk to?
- Can I follow up if I have more questions?

## Stakeholder Questions

### Business Context
- What problem are we trying to solve?
- Why is this important now?
- What happens if we don't solve it?
- How does this fit into company strategy?

### Success Criteria
- How will we know if this is successful?
- What metrics matter most?
- What's the timeline?
- What's the budget/resource constraint?

### Constraints
- What technical constraints exist?
- What regulatory or compliance requirements?
- What organizational dependencies?
- What has been tried before?

### Stakeholders
- Who are the key decision makers?
- Who needs to be informed?
- Who might block this?
- Who are the champions?

## Synthesizing Findings

### Affinity Mapping

1. Write each insight on a sticky note
2. Group similar insights together
3. Name each group (theme)
4. Identify patterns across groups

### Key Questions for Synthesis

- What themes appear across multiple interviews?
- What surprised us?
- What confirms our assumptions?
- What contradicts our assumptions?
- Where do users agree/disagree?

## Common Pitfalls

### Avoid These Mistakes

1. **Leading questions**: "Don't you think X would be better?"
   - Instead: "How do you feel about X?"

2. **Asking about future behavior**: "Would you use this?"
   - Instead: "Tell me about the last time you..."

3. **Solving during research**: Jumping to solutions too early
   - Stay in problem space during discovery

4. **Confirmation bias**: Only hearing what confirms your hypothesis
   - Actively seek disconfirming evidence

5. **Small sample size**: Drawing conclusions from 1-2 interviews
   - Aim for 5-8 to see patterns

6. **Not recording**: Relying on memory
   - Always take notes, record when possible
